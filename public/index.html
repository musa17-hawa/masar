Masar turns LiDAR scans and aerial photos into a 3D trail you can walk through on screen, with a report on the climb, the footing and the water along the way.

Scroll, and the trail builds itself.

Step 1

It starts as laser pulses

An aircraft, or the phone in your hand, sweeps the ground with LiDAR. Every pulse that comes back is one measured point. Millions of them, with no colour and no meaning yet.

Step 2

Ground, trees, rock

Masar sorts the points: what is ground, what grows on it, what was built on it. Take the trees away and the bare terrain underneath becomes a continuous surface.

  • Ground
  • Vegetation
  • Buildings
  • Water

Step 3

Colour from the air

An aerial image is laid over the terrain, and each point takes the colour of the pixel above it. The cloud starts to look like the place.

Step 4

The trail, measured

Upload a GPS track or draw the line yourself. Masar follows it across the model and measures the grade, the steps, the obstacles and the stream beds it crosses.

Step 5

Walk it at eye level

Drop to walking height and move along the trail. Look around at a hairpin, stop at a steep pitch, see what the footing is like before your boots are on it.

Step 6

Then read the report

Distance, climb, walking time and a difficulty rating, each traced back to the measurement it came from.

What you bring

One point cloud is enough to start. Everything else makes the result richer.

A LiDAR point cloud
Airborne survey tiles or a phone scan. LAS, LAZ, PLY or XYZ; zipped LAS straight from swisstopo works as it is.
An aerial image
A GeoTIFF, or a JPG or PNG with a world file. For Swiss coordinates Masar can fetch the image for you.
A GPS track
GPX, KML or GeoJSON from any hiking app. No track? Draw the trail on the model, or adopt a mapped path.
Or just a phone
Where no survey exists, scan the trail with a LiDAR iPhone. Masar coaches you through the capture step by step.

From LiDAR sensor to a product

Masar is a course project by a team of three. The brief: take raw sensor returns all the way to something a hiker can use.
